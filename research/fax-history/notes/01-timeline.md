## Era 1: copying telegraphs (1843–1900)

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1843 | British patent 9745 to Alexander Bain for an "Electric Printing Telegraph", dated 27 May; specification enrolled 27 November. Two pendulums, kept in step by clocks, scan a raised-metal message line by line; the receiver marks chemically treated paper. | Fax; Alexander Bain; web (Bain US patent 5,957) |
| 1846 | Bain reproduces graphic signs in the lab. In December he patents the chemical telegraph (282 words in 52 s on a Paris–Lille trial; Morse managed about 40 wpm). | Fax; Alexander Bain |
| 1847–48 ⚠ | Frederick Bakewell's "image telegraph" replaces pendulums with rotating cylinders. Insulating ink on metal foil; patent 1848. | Frederick Bakewell; Image scanner |
| 1848 | 5 December: Bain's US patent 5,957, for sending "copies of surfaces" between Boston and New York. | web |
| 1851 | Bakewell demonstrates his machine at the Great Exhibition, London. | Frederick Bakewell |
| 1856 | Giovanni Caselli shows a pantelegraph prototype to Leopold II, Grand Duke of Tuscany. | Pantelegraph |
| 1857–61 | Caselli builds the pantelegraph in Paris with engineer Paul-Gustave Froment. | Pantelegraph |
| 1858 | Becquerel demonstrates the pantelegraph at the French Academy of Sciences. | Pantelegraph |
| 1860 | 10 May: Napoleon III sees a demo. November: Paris–Amiens test (140 km) sends Rossini's signature. Rossini writes a piano piece, "allegretto del pantelegrafo". | Pantelegraph |
| 1861 | Caselli patents the pantelegraph; demo for King Victor Emmanuel II at the Florence Exhibition. | Giovanni Caselli |
| 1862 | 10 February: first "pantelegram", Lyon to Paris. | Pantelegraph |
| 1863 ⚠ | Public use of Paris–Lyon begins; Coopersmith dates the first commercial service to 1863. Paris–Marseille test (800 km). Four-month experimental line, London–Liverpool. | Pantelegraph; Giovanni Caselli; web (PopSci) |
| 1864 | French law formally admits the pantelegraph to the telegraph network. | Pantelegraph |
| 1865 | Regular Paris–Lyon service, usually called the first commercial fax service, 11 years before the telephone. | Fax |
| 1867 | Line extended to Marseille. Price: 20 centimes per cm² of image. About 5,000 faxes in the first year, peak 110 per hour. A Paris–Le Havre pantelegram survives from about this year. | Pantelegraph; Giovanni Caselli; web (Musée des arts et métiers) |
| 1870 | Service ends with the Franco-Prussian War. | Giovanni Caselli |
| 1875 | Alexander Graham Bell gets US patent 161,739 for an "autograph telegraph", a primitive fax. | Elisha Gray |
| 1876 | The telephone. | — |
| 1877 | Scientific American runs "Transmitting Photographs by Telegraph" (12 May, p. 297). | Fax, further reading |
| 1880–81 ⚠ | Shelford Bidwell's scanning phototelegraph: a selenium photocell inside a rotating cylinder. The first machine that could scan any 2D original. Published as "Tele-Photography" in Nature, 10 Feb 1881. | Shelford Bidwell |
| 1884 | Paul Nipkow patents the spiral-hole scanning disc. | Nipkow disk |
| 1887–88 | Elisha Gray starts work on the telautograph in 1887; patents granted 31 July 1888. It sends pen movements and first used digital pulses. | Telautograph; web (ETHW) |
| 1893 | Telautograph shown at the World's Columbian Exposition, Chicago; New York–Chicago link. | Telautograph; web (ETHW) |
| 1895 | Ernest Hummel's Telediagraph links the New York Herald, Chicago Times-Herald, St. Louis Republic, Boston Herald and Philadelphia Inquirer. Amstutz Electro-Artograph appears in Scientific American. | Wirephoto; Commons |
| 1896 | Account of Henry Sutton's "telephane" published. | Fax |
| c. 1900 | Foster Ritchie's analog "Telewriter" makes the telautograph practical. | Telautograph |

## Era 2: photo-telegraphy and wirephoto (1900–1940)

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1902 | Arthur Korn's "phototelautograph" scans with a selenium cell. | Image scanner |
| 1904 | Korn sends a photo Berlin–Nuremberg in 42 minutes. | web (ETHW) |
| 1906 | 17 October: Korn sends a photo of Crown Prince Wilhelm 1,800 km. | Arthur Korn |
| 1907 | Édouard Belin invents the Bélinographe (télestéréographe). Papers in Germany, France and England print photos sent by Korn's system in 12 minutes. His phototelegraph helps catch a thief from a Stuttgart bank in London. | Édouard Belin; Arthur Korn; web (ETHW) |
| 1908 | Korn's system sends a wanted-person photo from Paris to London. | Fax |
| 1911 | First amplitude modulator for fax patented, for telephone lines. A telautograph warns workers during the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire. | Radiofax; Telautograph |
| 1913 | Belinograph scans with a photocell and sends over ordinary phone lines. Korn sends a "cinematic recording" (Vienna). | Wirephoto; Arthur Korn |
| 1914 | Belinograph in use for news photos; a photo crosses by phone in about 4 minutes. | Édouard Belin; Commons |
| 1920 | Bartlane system (Harry Bartholomew, Maynard McFarlane) digitises photos for submarine cable: 5 grey levels on telegraph tape. Often called the first digital imaging system. | Bartlane system |
| 1921 | First Bartlane transatlantic picture (under 3 hours). Western Union sends its first photograph. Belin adapts his system for radio. | Bartlane system; Wirephoto; Édouard Belin |
| 1922 | RCA offers the first transatlantic facsimile service. | Radiofax |
| 1923 | Korn sends a picture of Pope Pius XI from Rome to Bar Harbor, Maine; published the same day in the New York World. Marvin Ferree announces "Telepix". | Arthur Korn; Radiofax; Wirephoto |
| 1924 | 19 May: AT&T sends 15 photos by telephone line, Cleveland to New York. Herbert Ives sends the first color fax (Rudolph Valentino, RGB separations). October: C. Francis Jenkins sends "radio-photo letters" to Senator Howell. Nyquist publishes "Certain Factors Affecting Telegraph Speed". | Fax; Herbert E. Ives; web (Radio News); Harry Nyquist |
| 1924 ⚠ | Late November: RCA photoradiogram (Richard Ranger) sends a photo of President Coolidge across the Atlantic. Wikipedia: New York → London, 29 Nov. Radio News (Feb 1925): public demo 30 Nov, London → New York. | Richard H. Ranger; web (Radio News) |
| 1925 | 1 January: Telepix makes the first coast-to-coast transmission. AT&T telephotography goes commercial. June: Jenkins broadcasts moving silhouettes by radio. | Wirephoto; Harry Nyquist; Charles Francis Jenkins |
| 1925–26 ⚠ | Rudolf Hell conceives the Hellschreiber (patent 1929). | Rudolf Hell; Radiofax |
| 1926 | RCA Radiophoto service begins. | Wirephoto; Radiofax |
| 1927 | Otho Fulton patents the Fultograph, a clockwork home receiver for radio pictures. First Siemens-Karolus-Telefunken picture service, Berlin to other European cities. | Fultograph; Radiofax |
| 1928 | Yasujiro Niwa's NE-type phototelegraph (NEC, completed May 1928) carries photos of Emperor Hirohito's enthronement from Kyoto to Tokyo for the Tokyo Nichi Nichi Shimbun in November. At least 19 picture-telegraph systems are in development worldwide, 4 commercial. German police adopt Korn's system. Nyquist publishes "Certain Topics in Telegraph Transmission Theory". Fultograph sells for £22 15s. | Yasujiro Niwa; web (IEEJ, NEC, ETHW); Arthur Korn; Harry Nyquist; Fultograph |
| 1929 | Hell patents the Hellschreiber. BBC broadcasts Fultograph images (759 programmes, 1929–32). Bartlane goes to 15 grey levels. Zworykin's improved wirephoto sends a page in about a minute. Lufthansa radios weather charts to planes in flight. | Rudolf Hell; Fultograph; Bartlane system; Wirephoto |
| 1930 | Public Tokyo–Osaka photo-telegraph service opens. | web (IEEJ, via search summary) |
| 1931 | 12 August: GE (Charles J. Young, Ernst Alexanderson) radio-faxes the Schenectady Union-Star to the liners America and Minnekahda; 15 minutes per page. | Radiofax |
| 1933 | First tests of the Finch Facsimile system, New Jersey. About 100 picture-transmission machines exist worldwide. | Radiofax; web (ETHW) |
| 1935 | AP Wirephoto service starts, fax's first profitable business (Coopersmith); its first photo is the December 1934 Adirondacks plane crash. 12 February: Noel Sickles's sketch of the USS Macon crash is the first drawing sent. William Finch founds his company. | Wirephoto; web (TAMU, Invention & Technology) |
| 1936 | International News Photos fields a portable wirephoto unit that needs only a long-distance phone line. | Wirephoto |
| 1937 | First radio-fax newspaper broadcast, Minneapolis–St Paul. RCA overnight service on KSD St. Louis. FCC experimental fax licences go to stations including WOR, WGN, WSM and WWJ (1937–38). | Radiofax; web (Radio World) |
| 1938 | WOR New York broadcasts a "wireless newspaper" to Finch home receivers. 7 December: St. Louis Post-Dispatch "Radio Edition" over KSD, printed on 15 receivers. | Fax; Nationaal Archief photos; web (Invention & Technology) |
| 1939 | W9XZY St. Louis delivers a daily newspaper by radio fax; over 1,000 US homes get experimental receivers. Crosley sells the "Reado" home receiver. | Radiofax; web |

## Era 3: radio fax, war and weather (1940–1970)

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1941–45 | Fax carries maps, orders and weather charts in WWII. The Iwo Jima flag-raising photo (Feb 1945) is radio-faxed Guam to New York, 12,781 km, and printed within a day. Hellschreiber used alongside Enigma. Wartime paper shortages end most radio-newspaper stations. | Radiofax; Hellschreiber; web (Radio World) |
| 1947 | Western Union tests the "Telecar" in Baltimore: a 1946 Plymouth with a two-way radio and a fax recorder. | web (Western Union Technical Review, 1951) |
| 1947 ⚠ | Muirhead fax machine (credited on Wikipedia to Alexander Muirhead, who died in 1920; it was his company, Muirhead & Co.). | Radiofax |
| 1948 | Western Union "Desk-Fax" (Garvice Ridings), a desktop telegram fax on spark-printer paper; over 40,000 in use during the 1950s. Radiofax receivers go into Telecar delivery vans. October: RCA Ultrafax sends *Gone with the Wind* (1,047 pages) in about two minutes at a Library of Congress demo. The Miami Herald runs a fax edition. | Fax; Radiofax; web (ETHW, Radio Age, Invention & Technology) |
| 1950s | US National Weather Service starts "weatherfax": land lines first, then HF radio. | Radiofax |
| 1951 | Baltimore Telecar fleet reaches 7 cars; each message takes about 65 s to print. | web |
| 1952 | Muirhead photo transmitters used by journalists at the Oslo Winter Olympics. Finch's company is bankrupt. | Commons; web |
| 1956 | CCITT formed (merger of CCIF and CCIT); it later writes the fax standards. | ITU-T |
| 1957 | Russell Kirsch's team at NBS makes one of the first digital scans (176 px wide, of his son Walden). Copthorne Macdonald proposes slow-scan TV (1957–58). | Image scanner; Slow-scan television |
| 1960s | Analog fax takes about 15 minutes a page; one page to the US costs 20–30 Deutschmarks (Musmann). The US Army sends the first satellite fax photo (Courier satellite, Deal Test Site to Puerto Rico). RCMP faxes mugshots and fingerprints. | web (Musmann oral history); Fax |
| 1964 | 5 May: Xerox announces the LDX (Long Distance Xerography): about 1,100 lb, 8 pages a minute, $800 a month, needs microwave, coax or special lines. | Fax; web (ETHW, Xerox) |
| 1966 | Xerox Magnafax Telecopier: 46 lb, any phone line, a letter page in about 6 minutes, rent from about $50 a month ("Mail letters over the phone!"). Soviet Luna 9 sends the first photos from the Moon's surface in radiofax format; Jodrell Bank decodes them for the Daily Express. | Fax; web (BizTech); Radiofax |
| 1968 | Carterfone decision: the FCC lets customer-owned devices connect to the AT&T network. CCITT Group 1 (T.2): analog, 6 minutes per page, little used. | Carterfone; Fax; web (IEEE milestone) |
| 1969 | Dacom files its run-length compression patent (US 4,135,214). Wikipedia credits Dacom's "Rapidfax" (correct name: Rapifax) as the first digital fax. | web (patent); Fax; Image scanner |
| 1970 | US installed base: about 25,000 fax machines. SRI starts a digital fax project for Savin, CBS and Dacom. | web (ETHW, SRI) |

## Era 4: the office fax boom (1970–2000)

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1972 | Japan revises its Public Telecommunications Act and opens phone lines to fax, quickly where the US opened gradually. | web (ETHW, Ricoh) |
| 1973 ⚠ | Ricoh RIFAX 600S, from the SRI work: digital, an A4 page in about a minute at 4,800 bit/s. Ricoh and SRI call it the first commercial digital fax. Tokyo–New York satellite demo in April. Sold in the US as Rapifax 100 from 1974. | web (Ricoh, SRI) |
| 1976 | CCITT T.3 Group 2: analog, 3 minutes per page ("three-minute facsimile"); first real cross-brand interoperability. The Group 3 coding work starts: IBM, KDD and the German PTT compete, and Modified Huffman is the compromise. | Fax; web (IEEE milestone, Musmann) |
| 1978 | Long-distance fax prices far below 1968 levels; Japanese firms crowd into the market. | Fax |
| 1980 | CCITT T.4 + T.30 Group 3: digital, Modified Huffman (MR optional), the "one-minute facsimile". At the CCITT Kyoto meeting Japan offers its Modified READ patents free if MR is adopted. US installed base: about 250,000. | Fax; web (IEEE milestone, ETHW) |
| 1981 | NTT launches its F-Net fax network. Sharp FO-2000 "HAYAX": 70 kg, A4 in 20 s at 9,600 bit/s, about ¥3 million. | web (IEEE milestone, ETHW) |
| 1983 | About 50,000 fax machines sold in the US. | web (CSM 1987) |
| 1984 | Group 4 (T.6, MMR) for 64 kbit/s ISDN. FedEx launches ZapMail; it loses over $300 million by 1986. | Fax; web (IEEE milestone, ETHW) |
| 1985 ⚠ | Hank Magnuski's GammaLink ships GammaFax, the first PC fax board. 12 January: Beuys, Warhol and Higashiyama "Global-Art-Fusion" fax art goes Düsseldorf–New York–Tokyo–Vienna in 32 minutes. US sales about 157,000; installed base about 500,000. | Fax; Fax art; web (Dataquest via CSM, ETHW) |
| 1986–88 | US sales climb: 191,000 (1986), about 424,600–500,000 (1987), 863,000 (1988). Junk fax arrives; telex declines. | web (CSM 1987, 1989); Junk fax; Telex |
| 1989 | A fax machine costs under $1,000, against $7,500–10,000 a decade earlier. A 20-page document costs $1.25 by fax and $14 by FedEx. June: after Tiananmen, Chinese students abroad fax Western news reports into China, and authorities start taking fax machines away. | web (CSM 1989) |
| 1990 | US installed base about 5 million, "nearly all made in Japan". | web (ETHW) |
| 1991 | 18 August (US time): a fax from a Yeltsin-government official reaches Washington, "It is Military Coup. Tanks are everywhere", before the first news reports. The US Telephone Consumer Protection Act bans unsolicited fax ads. | web (UPI); Junk fax |
| 1992 | CCITT renamed ITU-T. SupraFAXModem 14400 ships at $399. | ITU-T; Modem |
| 1993 | JBIG (T.82) bi-level compression standard. | JBIG |
| 1994 | V.34 approved for fax: 33.6 kbit/s "Super G3". | Fax |
| mid-1990s | Plain-paper fax (thermal transfer, inkjet, laser) replaces thermal paper. Email-to-fax services appear. | Fax; Internet fax |
| 1996 | July: analog T.2 and T.3 withdrawn as obsolete. | Fax |
| 1997 | US stand-alone fax sales peak at 3.6 million. | web (NYT 2005, via PopSci) |
| 1998 | T.38 fax-over-IP standard. | T.38 |
| 1999 | JBIG added to T.30. | Fax |
| 2000 | Japan's stand-alone fax sales peak. Email has made fax "obsolescent, though not obsolete" (Coopersmith). | web (ETHW) |

## Era 5: internet, decline and stubborn survival (2000–today)

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2004 | FCC fines Fax.com $5,379,000 for junk faxes, its first action against a "fax broadcaster". US fax sales fall to about 1.5 million. | web (FCC; NYT 2005) |
| 2005 | US Junk Fax Prevention Act adds an "established business relationship" exemption. | Junk fax |
| 2012 | 59% of Japanese households have a fax. IEEE dedicates the milestone "International Standardization of G3 Facsimile, 1980" at NTT Yokosuka. IEEJ honours the 1928 NE-type phototelegraph. | web (Cabinet Office via WaPo; ETHW; IEEJ) |
| 2017 | UK NHS described as the world's largest buyer of fax machines. | Fax |
| 2018 | NHS fax count put at 8,000–11,620. 9 December: the Health Secretary bans new purchases from January 2019, with removal by 31 March 2020. Two-thirds of Canadian doctors mainly use fax. The US CMS head calls for "fax free" doctors' offices by 2020. | Fax; web (gov.uk, RCS FOI) |
| 2020 | COVID-19 case reporting by fax causes errors and delays in Japan, Germany and the US. | Fax |
| 2021 | April: Japan's reform minister Taro Kono orders ministries to drop fax by June. More than 400 objections come back and the plan is abandoned. | web |
| 2023 | 67% of US hospitals still use fax (71% in 2018). | web (ONC data via TechTarget) |
| 2024 | Radiofax weather charts still broadcast to ships (Northwood, UK; NOAA; DWD; JMA). | Radiofax; Commons |
| 2025 | Japan still faxes widely; over 81% of convenience stores offer fax. Most NHS trusts report zero fax machines in FOI replies. | Fax; web (NHS FOIs) |
