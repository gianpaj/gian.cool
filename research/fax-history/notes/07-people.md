# People

Portraits are in `../images/people/` where a free image exists.

| Person | Dates | Role in the fax story | Portrait |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alexander Bain | 1810–1877 | Scottish clockmaker; 1843 patent for the first fax-like "electric printing telegraph"; also the electric clock and chemical telegraph. Lost a patent fight with Morse; died on a small pension. Emmy (posthumous, 2016). | `alexander-bain.jpg`, `alexander-bain-plaque-edinburgh.png` |
| Frederick Bakewell | 1799–1869 | English physicist; rotating-cylinder "image telegraph" (patent 1848), shown at the Great Exhibition of 1851. | — |
| Giovanni Caselli | 1815–1891 | Italian priest and physicist; pantelegraph; first commercial fax service (Paris–Lyon, 1865). Legion of Honour from Napoleon III. | `giovanni-caselli.png` |
| Paul-Gustave Froment | 1815–1865 | French engineer who built the pantelegraph with Caselli. | — |
| Shelford Bidwell | 1848–1909 | English barrister-physicist; first photocell scanner (1880–81); predicted TV's bandwidth problem. | — |
| Paul Nipkow | 1860–1940 | German inventor of the scanning disc (1884). | `paul-nipkow.jpg` |
| Elisha Gray | 1835–1901 | American inventor, Bell's telephone rival; telautograph (1888). | `elisha-gray.jpg` |
| Foster Ritchie | — | Gray's former assistant; made the analog telautograph ("Telewriter") practical, c. 1900. | — |
| Ernest A. Hummel | — | Telediagraph (1895), linking five US newspapers. | — |
| Arthur Korn | 1870–1945 | German physicist; selenium-cell Bildtelegraph; police and press photos 1906–1928; fled Nazi Germany in 1939. | `arthur-korn.jpg` |
| Édouard Belin | 1876–1963 | French inventor of the Bélinographe (1907); "belino" became the word for a wired photo. | `edouard-belin.jpg` |
| Harry G. Bartholomew & Maynard D. McFarlane | — | Bartlane digital cable-picture system (1920). | — |
| Herbert E. Ives | 1882–1953 | AT&T/Bell Labs; led telephotography (1924–25); first colour fax (1924); later TV and the Ives–Stilwell experiment. | `herbert-ives.jpg` |
| Harry Nyquist | 1889–1976 | Bell Labs; fixed picture-transmission distortion; 1924/1928 papers on signalling speed that fed information theory. | (only a fair-use photo exists; not downloaded) |
| Richard H. Ranger | 1889–1962 | RCA; transatlantic photoradiogram (1924). Later NBC chimes and tape recording (Oscar 1956). | `richard-ranger-rca-1921.jpg` (group photo) |
| C. Francis Jenkins | 1867–1934 | Radio pictures and mechanical TV; first US TV licence (W3XK, 1928). | `charles-francis-jenkins.jpg` |
| Otho Fulton | — | Fultograph home radio-picture receiver (1927–28). | — |
| Rudolf Hell | 1901–2002 | German engineer; Hellschreiber (1929); later weather-chart recorders, scanners and digital typesetting. | `../images/03-radio-fax-1920-1970/1968-rudolf-hell-weather-chart-recorder.jpg` |
| Yasujiro Niwa | 1893–1975 | NEC engineer; NE-type phototelegraph (1928), used for the enthronement photos Kyoto→Tokyo; first president of Tokyo Denki University; one of Japan's "Ten Great Inventors" (1985). | `yasujiro-niwa.jpg` |
| William G. H. Finch | b. 1897 | Born in Birmingham, England; INS engineer and FCC assistant chief engineer; Finch Facsimile radio newspaper (tested 1933, broadcast 1937–40s); 200+ patents; company bankrupt by 1952. | — |
| Garvice H. Ridings | — | Western Union engineer behind the Desk-Fax (1948), over 40,000 in use in the 1950s. | — |
| Charles J. Young & Ernst Alexanderson | — | GE radio fax to ships at sea (1931). | — |
| Vladimir Zworykin | 1888–1982 | Improved wirephoto (1929); better known for electronic TV. | — |
| Noel Sickles | 1910–1982 | AP artist; his USS Macon sketch was the first drawing sent by AP Wirephoto (1935). | — |
| David A. Huffman | 1925–1999 | Huffman coding (1952), the basis of fax compression. | — |
| Claude Shannon | 1916–2001 | Information theory (1948): the limit on how fast any line can carry data. | — |
| Russell A. Kirsch | 1929–2020 | Made one of the first digital scans (NBS, 1957). | — |
| Hank Magnuski | — | GammaLink founder; GammaFax, first PC fax board (1985). | — |
| Hans Georg Musmann | — | German engineer (TU Braunschweig) in the CCITT Group 3 coding work; his 1994 IEEE oral history explains the Modified Huffman compromise. | — |
| Thomas Huang | — | 1960s 2D binary-image coding that influenced Japan's Modified READ. | — |
| Taro Kono | — | Japanese reform minister whose 2021 order to drop fax in ministries was abandoned after 400+ objections. | — |
| Jonathan Coopersmith | — | Texas A&M historian; *Faxed: The Rise and Fall of the Fax Machine* (2015), co-winner of the 2016 Hagley Prize; short version on ETHW ("Fax Machines"). | — |
