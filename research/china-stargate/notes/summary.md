# Article summary and the Stargate name

Research checked on 2026-11-07. This summary covers the public preview of [SemiAnalysis's article](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/we-visited-chinas-stargate-in-inner). The detailed construction, cost and engineering sections are paywalled and were not read.

## Who is building it?

The site is Galaxy, an AI data centre campus in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, developed by Envision Group. Envision makes wind turbines, storage and energy systems. Its November announcement describes live computing workloads powered by its 800 V DC system. [Envision announcement, 2026-11-02](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/envision-achieves-worlds-first-commercial-deployment-of-integrated-800v-dc-sst-based-power-system-in-large-scale-ai-data-center-302926109.html)

"China's Stargate" is SemiAnalysis's comparison with the US project. Galaxy is not identified as an OpenAI project in the sources reviewed. OpenAI's US Stargate announcement names SoftBank, OpenAI, Oracle and MGX as initial equity funders, with SoftBank responsible for finance and OpenAI for operations. [OpenAI announcement, 2025-01-21](https://openai.com/index/announcing-the-stargate-project/)

## The article in brief

- SemiAnalysis visited Galaxy in September 2026. It reports a first phase of 120 MW delivered in August within a year, and a campus planned for up to 800 MW of IT capacity. Delivered capacity and planned capacity are different milestones.
- Envision supplies much of the energy infrastructure itself, including wind generation, batteries and electrical systems. The authors argue this integration helps it control construction speed and cost.
- The authors report seeing a live 5 MW pilot using solid-state transformers to convert 10 kV AC to an 800 V DC bus. Higher-voltage distribution is intended to serve increasingly power-hungry AI racks with fewer conversion stages and lower conductor losses. Actual site efficiency savings were not verified.
- The public preview also describes a shared cooling loop without a central chiller plant. The implementation detail is paywalled, and an independent equipment classification conflicts with this description.

The article's argument is that China's AI buildout has substantial engineering and manufacturing strengths, especially in the infrastructure that delivers power to computing equipment. It does not establish which country's AI models are better.

## What the evidence does not establish

The operating IT load, installed chip inventory and useful computing output remain unverified. Envision's larger capacity announcements also differ in scope from SemiAnalysis's 800 MW campus plan. Keep those differences visible rather than treating the largest announced number as capacity already running.
