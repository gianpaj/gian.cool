# Storyline options

Editorial suggestions from the research discussion. No storyline or running time has been selected for production.

The recommended length is about 3 to 3.5 minutes for a focused educational news video. Five minutes remains workable if the film gives viewers time to follow an engineering explanation. The existing [five-minute narrative backbone](narrative.md) is a research draft.

## 1. Why is a wind-turbine maker building an AI campus?

Suggested length: 3 to 4 minutes.

Opening line: "The company building this AI campus also makes the wind turbines that help power it."

Introduce Envision, follow electricity through batteries and power conversion to the racks, then explain why controlling that chain matters. End by distinguishing the first phase SemiAnalysis reports as delivered from the future campus plan.

This is the recommended angle. It gives viewers a concrete reason to care about the engineering and works with the collected wind, rack and cooling imagery. A brief voltage explanation from option 2 can supply the worked example.

## 2. The 800-volt experiment

Suggested length: 2.5 to 3 minutes.

Opening line: "As AI racks consume more power, even the wiring becomes a problem."

Begin with the reported 5 MW pilot. Explain how higher voltage reduces current for a given DC power, why that can reduce conductor losses, and how batteries help handle demand changes. Keep the arithmetic illustrative and separate from measured Galaxy performance. The worked example can carry the explanation as the handshake did in the fax video.

## 3. How big is China's "Stargate," really?

Suggested length: 2 to 3 minutes.

Opening line: "When someone announces a gigawatt of AI, what have they actually built?"

Use Galaxy to distinguish announced, delivered and operating capacity. Anchor the explanation in SemiAnalysis's reported 120 MW first phase and 800 MW campus plan. Explain why buildings and megawatts alone cannot establish installed chips or useful AI output.

Source-backed facts and qualifications for all three options are in [the evidence notes](evidence.md). Campus and article imagery needs permission; the licensed hardware photos illustrate mechanisms and do not show Galaxy's equipment.
