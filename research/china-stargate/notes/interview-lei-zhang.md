## Questions worth asking

1. You built Envision around energy. What made you decide to build the computing facilities that consume it?
2. SemiAnalysis describes an 800 MW IT campus, while Envision's announcements describe above 2 GW and then 3 GW. What site, phase and power definition does each figure cover? How much IT capacity is commissioned, fitted with hardware and drawing load today?
3. What is the measured renewable share at Galaxy, and over what period? Can you distinguish annual energy matching from hourly supply, and explain the role of grid imports during long wind lulls?
4. What does the 5 MW 800 V DC pilot actually power? Can your engineers provide measured conversion losses, availability and load-response results, with a conventional system for comparison?
5. What limits the next phase most: power connections, chips, networking, customers or financing? Which accelerator models and quantities can you confirm publicly?
6. SemiAnalysis describes no central chiller plant, while Epoch AI classifies equipment as air-cooled chillers. Can the site team identify the equipment and provide the cooling diagram, measured PUE and water-use figures?
7. Your desert-AI ambition includes fully renewable computing. Which project does that describe, what has already been demonstrated, and what remains a target?

For quantitative answers, ask for the reporting date, units, equipment boundary and underlying document. The capacity and cooling disagreements are questions to resolve, not accusations.

## Documents and imagery to request

- A dated phase schedule distinguishing design, energisation, customer acceptance and loaded operation.

- A permitted electrical diagram, cooling equipment identification and pilot test results.
- Written reproduction permission and high-resolution originals for Galaxy photographs, plus a portrait and any approved interview clips. Confirm creator credit and allowed uses; company supply alone does not settle every third-party right.
