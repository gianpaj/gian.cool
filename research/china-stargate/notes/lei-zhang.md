# Lei Zhang and the move from wind to AI infrastructure

Research date: 2026-11-07. Sources and access limits are in [the founder source register](../sources/lei-zhang.md). This is Lei Zhang of Envision, also written Zhang Lei. Search results include unrelated people with the same English name, including the Hillhouse investor.

## Why he belongs in this story

Lei Zhang is Envision's founder and CEO. He can explain the strategic decision to turn a wind and energy-systems company into an AI infrastructure developer. His role makes him a useful interview subject; it does not make him an independent verifier of the company's performance. [L1, L2, L5](../sources/lei-zhang.md)

The useful question is why an energy company wants to own computing infrastructure. The Galaxy report describes Envision supplying wind generation, storage and electrical integration alongside the campus itself. Zhang's public comments place that move within a wider ambition to locate computing near renewable resources. Connecting these statements to Galaxy is an editorial interpretation, not evidence that every desert-energy project is the same site. [S1–S3](../sources/README.md), [L6, L7](../sources/lei-zhang.md)

## What his statements can help test

His public ambition for wholly renewable desert computing raises a measurable question about Galaxy. SemiAnalysis reports an own-wind target above 60% for the first two phases, while Envision's November release gives an above-80% direct-renewables target for its larger announced buildout. Different sites, phases and accounting boundaries may explain the difference. Ask him to identify those boundaries before treating the statements as a contradiction. [S1, S3](../sources/README.md), [L7](../sources/lei-zhang.md)

The company homepage also promotes off-grid AI computing and a Chifeng energy system. That does not establish that Galaxy in Ulanqab is off-grid or entirely renewable-powered. Keep Chifeng, Galaxy and the wider Mission Gobi initiative distinct. [L1](../sources/lei-zhang.md), [S1–S3](../sources/README.md)

## Interview recommendation

Request Zhang for the strategy and the decision to enter AI infrastructure. Also request someone responsible for Galaxy's commissioning and power systems for the technical evidence. Envision's releases name Kevin Huang in connection with its power systems and Ricky Zheng with the AIDC business; these are potential technical interview leads, not confirmed participants. [S2, S3](../sources/README.md)

The opening question could be: "You built Envision around energy. What made you decide to build the computing facilities that consume it?"

For a short video, his explanation could introduce the wind-to-rack story. Keep the biography brief and use the interview to obtain specific facts, rather than turning the film into a founder profile. No interview has been requested or arranged. See [the interview brief](interview-lei-zhang.md).
