These are the steps, with the prompts I typed (typos included).

## 1. Research

> Can you start taking the history of the fax machine from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fax and related articles on Wikipedia and other places and compile them? Both images and text. [...] Make a few folders and organize the content and research that you find

Claude pulled 51 Wikipedia articles and 110 Wikimedia Commons images with their licences through the Wikipedia and Commons APIs, plus a few recordings: a fax handshake, a radiofax signal, modems. A second agent covered sources outside Wikipedia: books, IEEE pages and museums, plus 13 public-domain patent drawings and magazine scans. The output was folders of notes, a timeline, a fact-check list and a credits file.

## 2. Story

With the HyperFrames plugin installed in Claude Code:

> Using /hyperframes:hyperframes make a 5 min about the history fo the Fax machine

It pitched three storylines and recommended one: a single page travelling through every fax machine in history. I asked for a twist, using a 7-beat story map: hook, old way, new rule, mechanism, proof, payoff, action. The twist became the thesis: **the first fax was digital**. Bain's 1843 machine sent dots that were on or off. Fax went analog for a century to carry photos, and came back to dots in 1980.

![The first fax was digital. In 1843.](/2026-11-03-fax-history-video-first-fax-was-digital.webp)

## 3. Picture

> looks great. go ahead. only video first. we'll add the voice after

- A Python script turned the archive images into a fax look: 1-bit dots for anything after 1980, greyscale for the analog years. It also drew a spectrogram of the real fax handshake recording.
- Claude wrote a `SPEC.md` with every shot, its timing and its on-screen copy, plus a list of banned AI-video clichés.
- Claude built the first scene itself. Four subagents built the other 12 in parallel. Each scene is an HTML file animated with GSAP.
- `hyperframes check` caught layout and timing problems, and `hyperframes snapshot --review` made contact sheets of every scene and cut to look at.

![The scream, decoded](/2026-11-03-fax-history-video-fax-scream-decoded.webp)

## 4. Voice

> should we generate the speech now and add it to the preview? use gemini 3.8 tts

- Claude wrote a script of about 30 lines that explains around the on-screen text instead of reading it out.
- The HyperFrames audio engine generated the lines with `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` (voice Charon) and my Gemini API key.
- The first pass came to 284 seconds of speech, too long for the picture. Claude shortened 16 lines and regenerated them. It didn't stretch the scenes.
- Each line sits in its scene as its own `<audio>`. The fax and modem recordings play quieter under the voice.

## 5. Render

I hit render in HyperFrames Studio, which runs locally. Headless Chrome captured 9,000 frames, and FFmpeg encoded them with the audio in about 3 minutes.

No HeyGen account or cloud render was involved. The only costs were my Claude plan and the Gemini TTS calls.

## 6. Upload

Claude drafted the YouTube title, the chapters, and the description with credits for every CC BY-SA image and sound.

> generate or extract a thumbnail for the video

Claude didn't grab a frame from the film, because its text is too small to read at thumbnail size. It built a single still in HyperFrames with the same fonts and colours: Bain's 1843 drawing, cut by the orange scan line, with the half below the line turned into fax dots.
